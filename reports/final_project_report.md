## Executive decision summary

The accepted curated sales layer reports **£10,642,110.80** across **19,960
valid orders**, with an average order value of **£533.17**. The strongest
commercial signal is retention: **93.09% of attributed revenue** comes from
returning known customers. That signal must be paired with an attribution
workstream because guest orders still contribute **£1,754,901.91**, or
**16.49% of total valid revenue**.

Revenue accelerates from September and peaks in **November 2011 at
£1,503,866.78**. December is only the first nine days of the source period and
must not be treated as a complete-month decline. The United Kingdom generates
**£9,001,744.09 (84.59%)**, so geography is a material concentration risk.

Recommended operating posture: protect availability and retention around the
September–November peak, improve consent-based guest attribution, and validate
secondary-market economics before committing expansion spend.

## Decision recommendations

### 1. Prioritise retention experiments

Returning customers drive most attributed revenue. Test loyalty, replenishment,
and re-engagement journeys for identified customers, with treatment/control
measurement and incremental revenue as the success metric. The current data
does not contain campaign cost or causal lift, so ROI is not claimed here.

### 2. Convert guest demand into attributable relationships

Guest orders are 16.49% of total valid revenue. Improve consent-based account
capture and customer identification at checkout, then track guest-revenue share
and attributed revenue without forced or duplicate identities. This protects
the interpretation of the 93.09% returning-customer share.

### 3. Prepare for the seasonal peak

Revenue reaches £1,056,435.19 in September, £1,151,263.73 in October, and
£1,503,866.78 in November. Plan inventory, staffing, and marketing capacity
for the complete-month peak window. Refresh the source before setting a
December target.

### 4. Reduce geographic concentration deliberately

The United Kingdom accounts for 84.59% of valid revenue. Prioritise secondary
markets with observed demand, but validate product fit, shipping economics,
tax requirements, and retention before scaling spend.

### 5. Investigate cancellations before claiming recovery

The 16.12% cancellation metric is an invoice-count rate. Match cancellation
invoices to original invoices and quantify net revenue impact, units returned,
and recoverable stock before using it as a value-recovery KPI.

## Reliability, assumptions, and known risks

The accepted quality handoff records **6 PASS, 5 WARN, and 0 FAIL**. Warnings
are retained audit characteristics:

- Exact duplicates are reported and removed before the cleaned layer.
- Missing customer IDs remain in sales totals but are excluded from customer
  repeat-rate denominators and the attributed-revenue denominator.
- Negative quantities and non-positive prices remain in the audit layer but are
  excluded from curated positive sales.
- Line-value IQR outliers are flagged, not silently removed.
- Cancellation invoices are identified by an `InvoiceNo` prefix of `C` but are
  not matched back to original invoices in the current cancellation rate.
- The source has no tax, shipping, currency, or product-category fields;
  revenue is GBP line value and product analysis is at StockCode/description
  grain.
- Customer keys are salted, truncated SHA-256 digests. They are pseudonymous
  identifiers, not a guarantee of anonymity.
- The source ends on **2011-12-09**, making December a partial month.

These limitations constrain causal, net-return, category, and cross-currency
claims. They do not invalidate the accepted descriptive KPIs when their stated
denominators are used.
